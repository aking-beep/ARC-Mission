# Notes — ARC Mission (exp-2026-0007)

## 2026-11-21

- Built before any nonprofit saw it. That reverses the record's original plan (snapshots
  by hand first), by the owner's decision. Everything here is unvalidated.
- Chose rules over a model for findings. A leader can check "58% from one source" against
  their own books; they cannot check a paragraph a model wrote. Trust first.
- The sample organization scores 52 "stable" while carrying two act-now risks. The single
  number undersells the risks sitting under it — watch whether real leaders read the score
  and stop. If they do, drop the score and lead with findings.
- Mission Funding is the weakest module: without live listings it is advice, not
  discovery. It is the one most likely to need real data to be worth anything.

## 2026-11-21 (later) — funding and transformation built out

- Researched 16 technology programs against their official pages rather than from
  memory, and it mattered: Microsoft's free Business Premium grant is gone, Zoom is no
  longer on TechSoup, Atlassian renamed and changed its offer in February 2026.
- Could not get real grant listings into an offline tool honestly. Chose deep links into
  the databases that hold them over a fake-looking list. Only three search URL patterns
  could be confirmed; the rest open the database's front page.
- The sample organization qualifies for all 16 programs. That is a wall of cards, and
  the first thing a leader will ask is "which three first?" — the tool does not say.
- "ARC can help" now appears on roadmap steps, but nothing is behind it: no intake, no
  contact, no price. The brief is text on a clipboard. This is the gap between the
  platform and the practice, now visible on screen.
- GrantConnect's terms restrict using its materials to drive traffic. We only link to its
  front page; check before doing anything more with it.
